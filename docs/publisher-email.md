# Publisher & distributor outreach: email template

A ready-to-send pitch for game publishers, platform curators, streamers and
distributors. The bracketed parts are the ones to fill in before sending.

Everything the mail points at is public: the website, the repository, the
Discord and the video. There is no attachment and no sign-up.

---

**Voidborn in one line:** two armies of surreal alien monsters fight over

encyclopedia and the running servers, so it is the page a player or a curator will actually land on.

### What it is

* A complete RTS economy lifted off the ground: a commander, metal extractors,
  energy, factories, defences, radar and storage, with every mobile unit
  flying at its own altitude. Layers are real — a scout at 60 elmos and a
  siege walker at 230 are in different places, and that decides fights.
* Sixteen mobile units across two factions — the pale, long-ranged **Choir of
  the Eye** and the cheap, tough, short-reaching **Maw Swarm** — plus eight
  structures each, a commander with a manual-fire super weapon, and bots at
  three skill levels so a match can be watched without a second player.
* Eleven arenas: the official Voidborn Rift and ten more in the map repository,
  from a two-player duel to a twenty-player free-for-all.

### The part that is unlike anything else

Every RTS lets you build the units the designer made. Voidborn lets you make
your own, **in the game, while it is running**:

* pick any unit as the base and change its health, speed, range, damage,
  reload, sight, altitude, costs and stance with sliders and toggles;
* build it a **new 3D body** out of primitives — box, sphere, cylinder, cone —
  with size, position, rotation, colour and the part of a picture mapped onto
  it, with a live preview;
* the game then **prices it for you**: the stronger the design, the more metal
  and the more build time it costs, calculated from the base unit's own cost,
  so a cheap unit stays cheap and a strong one is taxed hard;
* and it queues like a real unit: per-factory production lines that build in
  parallel, a factory that is still under construction waits for the unit it
  was ordered, hold and cancel, refunds.

Units also grow with experience and split in two when they have earned it, so a
design you made keeps fighting after you made it.

That is the hook, and it is the thing the video shows: <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TtKTK60Kb-I>
(26 minutes, no commentary).

### Why it is worth a publisher's attention

* **It is free and open source, with no monetisation inside it.** No accounts,
  no ads, no in-game store, no launcher of our own. Publishing it does not
  cannibalise anything, and the repository is the marketing: the website, the
  video, the code and the Discord are all the same project.
* **The engine is not a risk.** It runs on Spring/Recoil, the engine Beyond All
  Reason uses, so the players, the lobby (SkyLobby) and the toolchain already
  exist. It is a game, not a mod, with its own start scripts.
* **There is a visible future.** A third faction, factory upgrades from tier 1
  to tier 2, and unit evolution with proliferation control are all in
  development, along with hosted games and a replay archive — the kind of
  roadmap a publisher can plan a release against.
* **It fits the audience that already exists.** The people who play
  Beyond All Reason, Zero-K and Spring are exactly the people who would want
  to build their own overpowered unit and see it split in two.

### Where it honestly stands

It is an early first pass, and I would rather say so than have you find out
later. The engine run is verified end to end and the game is playable, but the
balance is a first pass, the bot is simple, and the look and feel has not been
polished on real hardware. That is what the roadmap is for, and it is why a
publisher, a curator or a platform would genuinely move it forward.

## Notes for the sender

* **Who to send it to.** Platform curators and community managers first
  (they decide what gets a store page or a video), then streamers who cover
  Spring, Zero-K or BAR. Publishers who already work with open-source games
  are a good fit, because the licence is the thing they usually ask about.
* **Keep the subject line.** It names the engine and the hook; that is the
  whole pitch in one line.
* **Do not attach anything.** The website is the showcase and it is fast: the
  pages load nothing from the network and the whole site is under 2 MB.
* **Mention the video.** It is the fastest way to show what the forge does,
  and it needs no explanation.
* **One ask per mail.** Ask for Steam interest in one mail and for coverage in
  another; both in one mail is how a pitch gets ignored.
* **Follow up once.** If there is no answer after two weeks, one short line
  asking whether the pitch reached the right person is enough.
