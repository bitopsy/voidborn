THE STORY

Everything about Voidborn in one place: what happened at the Rift, who is fighting there, which units are worth knowing by name, every option the game has, every key it answers to – and the plan for the day the players’ own units become part of the game itself.

The Rift

The void is not empty. It is full – of the dark that runs between the stars, of the light that leaks out of them, and of the things that learned to live in both.

Long before anything with a name walked the asteroids, a wound opened in the dark. Not a hole: a Rift. Where it touches a world, matter starts to wake up. Stone grows eyes. Water learns to hold a shape. The first things that moved in the Rift were the ones that could see, because the ones that could not see were the ones the light found first.

Five of those things are still here, and they have been killing each other over the same asteroids for a very long time. They are the only kinds of life the Rift made that survived long enough to build anything at all – and the difference between them is not what they are made of, but which sense they decided to trust.

The Rift is also why you are there. There is metal in the asteroids, energy in the seams, and something older than both in the quiet places between them. It is a good place to build an army. It is a bad place to be alone.

Five ways to meet the dark

The five armies do not disagree about the Rift. They disagree about which of the senses to trust in it: the eye, the mouth, the ear, the nose or the hand.

The Choir of the Eye

A single enormous eye, split into a hundred smaller ones.

The Choir sees. That is the whole of it, and it is enough: the Choir of the Eye builds its strength out of vision. Where the Choir looks, the world becomes information, and information becomes power. Their units are foundations and lenses and patient, watching shapes; they win by knowing where you are before you know where they are.

They are also very sure of themselves. The Hierophant, their commander, is a cyclops that builds, sees everything, and burns whatever it stares at for too long. The Choir does not chase. It waits, and then it is already behind you.

Strength: information, area control, artillery that never has to reload.

Weakness: expensive, slow, and poor at holding ground it did not look at first.

The Maw Swarm

A mouth that learned to make more mouths.

The Maw does not see. It grows. Every Maw thing is a stomach with an appetite, and the whole swarm is one animal that happens to be made of many small ones. What the Maw loses, it regrows in front of your eyes; what it kills, it eats. A Maw army does not have a position, it has a digestion.

Their commander, the Matriarch, is the largest thing in the hive: it spits acid, it devours whole batteries with a single beam, and it is the reason the Maw never really loses – it just gets thinner. The Maw is cheap, it is numerous, and it is everywhere the Choir is not looking.

Strength: numbers, cheap replacements, and the ability to come back from anything.

Weakness: fragile units, slow to turn around, and nothing at all in open sight.

The Tympan Conclave

An ear that learned to answer.

The Conclave listens. In the dark of the Rift the first thing to arrive is not light but sound, and the Conclave turned that into a doctrine: hear first, strike first, strike together. Its units see little and hear a great deal – scouts, support units and the commander carry radar of their own – and what they hear they hit with waves of pressure that break more than they pierce.

Alone, a Tympan unit is ordinary. Together it is not: each of them that hits the same target within a moment of the others makes every later hit ring louder. Their ears also give away the quiet: an enemy commander that cloaks too close to a Conclave listener hears its own cloak go.

Strength: focus fire, long warning, area damage, and nothing hides from them for long.

Weakness: short sight, splash that does not care where it lands, and every unit alone is only a unit.

The Miasma Bloom

A nose that learned to bloom.

The Bloom smells. It does not need to see what it has tasted: every shell, dart and jet it lands leaves a poison that works for seconds after the shot and a scent that keeps the target on the Bloom’s map for ten. Its units are slow, soft-looking and hard to kill, and what they leave behind is a cloud nobody wants to stand in.

The Bloom feeds on endings. The Censer drifts over the wreckage and eats the mushrooms that grow from it faster than anyone else, and its commander, the Rhinarch, is a drifting snout in a crown of petals that breathes poison across half the field.

Strength: damage that keeps working, no hiding place, and a lot of health for the metal.

Weakness: slow, and short on reach in the early game; poison does nothing to a unit that is already dying.

The Haptic Grasp

A hand that learned to hold on.

The Grasp touches. Where the others trust what reaches them across the dark, the Grasp trusts only what it can feel – and it has the shortest sight and the shortest reach of any army in the Rift. Its answer is to close the distance. Hit a target from point-blank range and a Grasp unit does up to seventy-five percent more damage.

Its raiders do not shoot at all. The Clutch and the Strangler are kamikaze units: they cross the gap, grip, and do not let go. Behind them come hands and tentacles of every size, up to the Atlas, a ninety-five thousand point titan that fights with a Grasping Beam, a storm of nerves and a fist.

Strength: devastating damage at point-blank range, and raiders that cost almost nothing.

Weakness: the shortest sight and reach in the game; a Grasp army that cannot close the distance does very little.

The ones worth knowing

Each side has twenty-one units and a commander. These are the ones that decide a match.

The Choir

Hierophant

Commander · 4200 HP · 2500 metal

Builds, sees the whole map, and stares hard enough to set things on fire. It is also the only unit that can fire the Destabilisation Gun: a shell that lands and spreads a wave of electric paralysis across the ground.

Panopticon

Heavy · 34000 HP · 4200 metal

The Choir’s answer to anything that gets close. Verdicts targets at 800 and watches them with a hundred eyes at 480. It is the unit you build when the Maw is already inside your base.

Sclera Knight

Assault · 4200 HP · 520 metal

The tier two assault unit: a Knight Gaze that reaches 430 and a Lash Volley at 330. It is the unit that wins the middle of the map, and the tier two factory is the decision that decides whether you get one.

Lidless Lance

Artillery · 1100 HP · 640 metal

A Lance Beam that reaches 1150 – further than anything else on the field, and it hits the whole time. The Choir does not need to see to use it, which is the point.

Singularity Heart

Energy · 3600 HP · 1100 metal

Where the Choir’s energy comes from. Lose it and the second tier stops being a plan.

Iris Womb → Great Iris Womb

Factory · 3300 → 7200 HP

The Choir’s factory, and its upgrade. Select it and press Upgrade T2: 2000 metal and 2800 energy over 45 seconds, and it turns in place into the Great Iris Womb with everything it was building still in the queue.

The Maw

Matriarch

Commander · 4400 HP · 2500 metal

The Hive’s mother: acid at 420, a Devour beam at 240 that eats 2600 points of armour, and the same Destabilisation Gun the Choir has.

Devourer

Heavy · 46000 HP · 3900 metal

The Maw’s siege piece: a Maw Cannon at 700 and a Brood Barrage at 600, behind 46000 points of health. It is the single largest thing in the game and it moves like it is being carried.

Warmaw

Assault · 5200 HP · 480 metal

The tier two assault unit. A Siege Bolt at 400 and a Spore Pod at 380 – the pod does something the Choir has no answer to at all.

Rupturer

Artillery · 1300 HP · 620 metal

A Bile Salvo that reaches 1180. The Maw’s answer to the Lance, and it is cheaper.

Heartfire Egg

Energy · 3900 HP · 1050 metal

The Maw’s power. It is also the thing the whole swarm is trying to protect.

Brood Hive → Great Brood Hive

Factory · 3500 → 7600 HP

The Maw’s factory and its upgrade. Same rules, same price: 2000 metal and 2800 energy over 45 seconds, and the production lines move across with it.

The Tympan Conclave

Auditor

Commander · 4300 HP · 2500 metal

Builds, carries radar and ears, and answers with a Crescendo at 520. It fires the same Destabilisation Gun as the others, and its Perforate takes 2600 points of armour.

Whisper

Scout · 220 HP · 40 metal

Radar to 1500 and ears to 450: it finds the enemy long before it sees it, and blows the cloak of anything that hides nearby.

Drummer

Assault · 1500 HP · 190 metal

A Bass Wave that reaches 360 with a wide splash. A pack of them, all on one target, is the Conclave’s middle of the map.

Trumpeter

Artillery · 620 HP · 235 metal

A Sonic Boom at 800, from one colossal ear-trumpet; with radar of its own it fires at what it has only heard.

Cacophon

Heavy · 9800 HP · 1600 metal

A Shockwave at 600 and the Lesser Voices at 380. It does not win a duel, it wins the fight round it.

Organon

T2 Heavy · 35000 HP · 4200 metal

A Requiem at 780 and a Hundred Voices at 470: fifteen pipes and one chord. The unit the Grand Echo Chamber exists for.

The Miasma Bloom

Rhinarch

Commander · 4700 HP · 2500 metal

Noxious Breath at 520, Asphyxiate at 240 and the Destabilisation Gun. Everything it hits is poisoned and scented.

Sniffer

Scout · 260 HP · 34 metal

Long sight (680) and a Spore Dart that poisons what it touches: a scout that marks its target.

Bellows

Assault · 2300 HP · 170 metal

A pleated barrel that heaves Fume Bolts at 340. Cheap, tough, and the poison keeps working after it has gone.

Stinkhorn

Artillery · 700 HP · 215 metal

A Reek Canister at 800 with a splash of 100: the cloud outlasts the blast.

Censer

Support · 820 HP · 150 metal

Builds, repairs and eats mushrooms about a third faster than the other first-tier supports.

Titan Arum

T2 Heavy · 48000 HP · 3800 metal

The corpse flower grown to a monster: Putrefaction at 700 and a Corpse Cloud at 600, behind the most health in the game.

The Haptic Grasp

Manus

Commander · 4400 HP · 2500 metal

The hand at the head of the Grasp. It builds the army and starts the game like every commander, and like every Grasp unit it sees little: the plan is to hold on to what is close.

Clutch

Raider (kamikaze) · 300 HP · 55 metal

The cheapest thing in the army, and it does not shoot. It crosses the gap, grips, and does not come back.

Strangler

T2 raider (kamikaze) · 900 HP · 150 metal

The Clutch at three times the health: the tier two kamikaze, for the targets a first wave does not get through.

Fist

Assault · 1700 HP · 190 metal

The first-tier assault unit, and the reason a Grasp army starts a fight at point-blank range, where the closeness bonus is at its largest.

Hecatoncheir

T2 heavy · 38000 HP · 4050 metal

The hundred-handed: the tier two heavy of the Grasp, built to stand in the middle of the other side’s army and stay there.

Atlas

T3 heavy · 95000 HP · 9800 metal

The Grasp’s tier three heavy, the only unit a Titan Palm adds: a Grasping Beam at 640, a Nerve Storm of lightning at 380 and a Titan Fist at 520.

The small ones matter too. The Lashling and the Gnasher are your first army and your last: a 330 or 560 point raider that costs almost nothing. The Skitter and the Gazer are your eyes. The Nurse, the Weeper and the Midwife are the ones that build, repair and reclaim – and the Midwife builds twice as fast as anything else in the game.

How a match actually goes

The commanderBuilds, sees everything, and never dies on its own. Lose it and the team is out.
The factoryBuilds your army. Upgrading it to tier two costs 2000 metal and 2800 energy.
ExperienceUnits that fight grow. At 0.14 they split in two, and growth doubles their size first.
The forgeDesign your own unit – shape, stats, weapons – and build it through the queue.

The Destabilisation Gun

Both commanders carry a third weapon: the Destabilisation Gun. It fires a slow shell at a spot of your choosing, for 1000 energy. Where the shell lands, a wave spreads out across the ground like fire on dry grass.

Every enemy the wave touches is destabilised: it wriggles inside an electric shield, and it cannot move or shoot while the shield is on it. How long it takes to recover depends on how close it was to the impact – nine seconds at the centre, where the wave hits hardest, down to about one second out at the rim where it barely touched. The wave reaches 240 elmos and takes about four fifths of a second to get there.

It is a weapon for breaking a push, not for winning a fight: it does no damage at all. Its value is the seconds it takes back.

Gray matter and mushrooms

When a unit dies it does not simply vanish. It shrinks into a lump of gray matter over about two seconds, and the lump leaves something behind that grows mushrooms – eight different shapes, all drawn in code, no model files. The mushrooms hold energy, and a builder that can reclaim eats them like any other feature. Left alone a patch fills up on a curve: it holds the dead unit’s maximum health divided by fifteen, and never more than that. A Nurse takes about fifty energy a second off a patch, and a half-eaten patch grows back.

The unit forge

The forge is the reason this game exists. You design a unit: its body, its size, its health, its speed, its weapons, its price – and then you build it through the production queue like anything else. A stronger design costs more metal and takes longer to build, so the forge cannot be used to cheat the economy. It is open to every player in multiplayer, and by default it stays locked until you have upgraded your factory to tier two.

The day the players’ units become official

Right now a design you make in the forge is yours, on your machine, in your matches. The next step is that it can also be part of the game – and that your name is on it.

What it would mean

Submit a design to the project and it becomes an official unit: it is in the game for everybody, it is in the unit encyclopedia, it has its own picture and its own entry in the unit list – and it carries your name as its author. The same way a piece of music carries the name of the person who wrote it.

You would keep the right to your design: it is yours, credited to you, and it is published under the same free licence as the rest of the game so that anyone can build on it. In exchange the project gets a roster that keeps growing without the project having to design every unit itself – and the game gets a community that can point at its own creature in the encyclopedia.

How it would work

  1. You design it in the forge, exactly as you do now: body, stats, weapons, price. Save it.
  2. You submit it – a form in the game, or the Discord – with a name, a description and how you want to be credited.
  3. It is looked over: it has to fit the game (a price that makes sense, a role that does not exist yet, a balance that is not absurd), and it has to not be a copy of something that is already there.
  4. It is released with your name on it, in the next version, in the encyclopedia, with the original design kept in the repository and a link back to you.
What this needs from you. Nothing yet – this is the plan, not a job. But the whole point of it is that the roster comes from the people who play. If you would like to be in the first batch of credited unit authors, say so in the Discord. The roadmap has the rest of what is coming.
